# Management interpretation

## Executive readout

The model indicates recurring-revenue growth accompanied by continued cash consumption.

- June 2026 MRR reached **$430K**, equivalent to **$5.16M ARR**.
- Year-to-date revenue is **$59K above budget**, or roughly **2.5% favorable**.
- June GRR is **98.4%** and NRR is **98.4%**. In plain language, the existing customer base is slightly shrinking after churn and expansion are combined. New customer acquisition is carrying the topline.
- LTV/CAC is **5.7x**, which supports continued acquisition investment if the retention profile remains stable.
- The business generated a modeled **$3.57M YTD operating loss**. Cash falls from **$4.61M in June to $0.93M in December** in the base forecast.
- The Monte Carlo model places a **59.7% probability of ending below $1M**, while the probability of ending below zero is only **0.1%**. The immediate issue is funding optionality and operating buffer, not imminent insolvency.
- The rolling revenue back-test has **0.9% MAPE**, so the near-term revenue forecast is reasonably calibrated relative to this synthetic history. Liquidity remains more sensitive to hiring and cost timing than to revenue forecasting error.

## What management should ask next

1. **Retention:** Which plan, channel, and customer segment explains the sub-100% NRR? A topline growth target should not substitute for a retention plan.
2. **Hiring:** Which planned hires are tied to capacity, quota coverage, or control requirements? The scenario model shows that faster hiring can reduce near-term cash even when revenue growth improves.
3. **Cloud economics:** Is cloud/API pressure driven by usage growth, vendor price, or architecture? Those require different actions.
4. **Funding:** What minimum cash buffer does management require, and what financing lead time should trigger action?

## Interpretation guardrails

This is a synthetic portfolio case. LTV uses a simplified gross-margin-over-churn approach, the cash flow is a planning proxy rather than an audited GAAP cash-flow statement, and the Monte Carlo simulation does not model financing responses or correlated shocks. The numbers are useful for demonstrating FP&A reasoning, not for making a real investment decision.
